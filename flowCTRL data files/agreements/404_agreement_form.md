### 2. Purpose of Agreement

The purpose of this Agreement is to establish clear, fair, and binding mutual expectations among student developers, engineers, designers, and contributors collaborating on offline projects under the guidance, mentorship, or umbrella of the 404 Community. This agreement prevents ambiguity regarding project ownership, intellectual property, contribution credits, project continuity, and community integrity.

---

### 3. Role of 404 Community

1. **Facilitator & Incubator:** The 404 Community (*From Error to Execution*) operates as an offline peer-learning ecosystem, incubator, and technical community. It provides mentorship, peer code review, architecture guidance, and collaboration spaces.
2. **No Ownership Claim:** The 404 Community does **not** claim commercial ownership or equity in member-created projects merely by virtue of hosting, incubating, or mentoring the project team, unless a separate, explicit written agreement is executed.
3. **Neutral Mediator:** In case of peer disputes or deadlock, designated 404 Community mentors or advisors may act as neutral mediators upon request by team members.

---

### 4. Project Ownership

1. **Collective Team Ownership:** Unless otherwise agreed in an explicit written addendum signed by all core contributors, the project, its source code, architecture, and associated assets are owned collectively by the active contributors in proportion to their documented contributions.
2. **No Unilateral Transfer:** No single contributor, including the project lead, may unilaterally sell, relicense, assign, or transfer ownership of the collaborative project or its codebase to a third party without the unanimous written consent of all active contributors.

---

### 5. Contribution and Credit

1. **Transparent Attribution:** Every contributor who writes code, designs interfaces, authors documentation, conducts testing, or provisions infrastructure has the inalienable right to receive accurate credit for their specific contributions.
2. **Contributors Registry (`CONTRIBUTORS.md`):** Every project repository must maintain an active, accurate `CONTRIBUTORS.md` file or attribution registry documenting the names, roles, GitHub handles, and specific areas of contribution for each member.
3. **Prohibition of Credit Erasure:** Erasing, diminishing, or falsely reassigning credit of any past or present contributor is strictly prohibited and constitutes a material breach of this community agreement.

---

### 6. Mandatory 404 Community Attribution

All projects incubated, launched, or developed within the offline 404 Community must include visible community attribution:

1. **Repository Notice:** The project `README.md` must clearly display:  
   `"Proudly incubated within 404 Community — From Error to Execution"`
2. **User Interface / Footer / About Section:** Publicly deployed applications, web pages, or prototypes must include attribution to **404 Community** in their footer, about modal, or documentation.
3. **Official Community Mark:** Teams may display the official 404 Community logo, banner, and links to verified community channels.

---

### 7. Repository and Access

1. **Central Hosting:** Code must be maintained in a verified version control repository (e.g., GitHub, GitLab) under the team or designated community organization.
2. **Access Control & Permissions:** Active contributors must be granted appropriate access levels (Maintainer, Developer, or Contributor). 
3. **Zero Lockout Policy:** No team member may revoke another active contributor's repository access or lock members out of communication channels without following the formal dispute protocol outlined in Section 15.
4. **Credential Stewardship:** Shared API keys, environment credentials, and hosting logins must be securely managed via approved credential managers or repository secrets, and handed over cleanly upon member transitions.

---

### 8. Open Source / Private Project

1. **License Declaration:** At the inception of the project, the team must explicitly declare whether the project will be:
   - **Open Source:** Governed by an approved open-source license (such as MIT, Apache 2.0, or GNU GPL v3).
   - **Private / Proprietary:** Kept private during initial development, research, or stealth incubation.
2. **License Transition:** Transitioning a private project to open source (or vice versa) requires the supermajority consent (at least 75%) of all active contributors.

---

### 9. AI and Third-Party Resources

1. **Permissible AI Tooling:** Contributors are permitted to use AI coding assistants, language models, and developer intelligence tools (e.g., Gemini, OpenAI, Claude, GitHub Copilot) to assist with ideation, drafting, and debugging.
2. **License Compliance:** All third-party libraries, frameworks, open-source packages, models, and assets integrated into the project must strictly comply with their respective third-party licenses.
3. **Prohibition of Contaminated Code:** Contributors must never import leaked source code, proprietary enterprise secrets, or copyrighted materials that violate third-party intellectual property into the project repository.

---

### 10. Confidentiality

1. **Private Development:** If a project is designated as Private/Stealth, team members agree not to leak unreleased source code, proprietary algorithms, client data, or internal strategies outside the project team and community mentors.
2. **Respect for Peer Privacy:** Personal contact information, offline attendance records, and private community discussions must remain confidential and protected.

---

### 11. Public Presentation and Portfolio Use

1. **Portfolio Rights:** All contributors have the affirmative right to showcase the project, screenshots, system architecture, and demo videos in their personal portfolios, resumes, LinkedIn profiles, and job applications.
2. **Accurate Role Representation:** When presenting the project publicly or in interviews, contributors must accurately describe their specific role (e.g., "Frontend Developer", "Backend Architect", "UI/UX Designer") rather than claiming sole authorship of collaborative multi-member work.
3. **Hackathon / Competition Representation:** If presenting the project at hackathons, conferences, or college expos, the team must register under the verified project name and attribute the 404 Community.

---

### 12. Commercialization and Revenue

1. **Pre-Revenue Consensus:** If a project transitions from an educational/community prototype into a revenue-generating product, SaaS platform, or venture-backed entity, the contributors must draft and unanimously sign a formal Commercialization Addendum prior to accepting revenue or investments.
2. **Fair Revenue Split:** Commercial returns, cash prizes, grants, or monetization revenue must be distributed according to recorded, agreed-upon contribution milestones.
3. **Non-Extractive Community Policy:** The 404 Community takes 0% equity and 0% royalty from community projects by default, maintaining its mission as an educational catalyst.

---

### 13. Project Abandonment

1. **Definition of Abandonment:** A project is deemed inactive or abandoned if no substantive code commits, progress updates, or team meetings occur for sixty (60) consecutive days, and the project lead fails to respond to community inquiries within fourteen (14) days.
2. **Succession Protocol:** If a project is abandoned:
   - Remaining interested contributors may elect a new project lead to continue development.
   - If all original contributors depart, the 404 Community may preserve, archive, or open-source the codebase for educational use by future cohorts, preserving all original author credits.

---

### 14. Leaving the Project

1. **Notice Requirement:** Any contributor wishing to depart the project must provide at least fourteen (14) days' written notice to the team lead and community chapter coordinator.
2. **Orderly Handover:** The departing member must:
   - Commit and push all outstanding local work and documentation.
   - Transfer any accounts, API keys, hosting credentials, or domain names under their personal name to the active team.
3. **Credits Retained:** Departing contributors retain full attribution in `CONTRIBUTORS.md` for work completed prior to their departure, but forfeit future operational decision-making power on post-departure milestones.

---

### 15. Disagreements and Resolution

1. **Direct Peer Dialogue:** In the event of a dispute regarding roles, technical architecture, or contribution splits, team members must first engage in good-faith direct discussion.
2. **Community Mediation:** If direct discussion does not resolve the issue within seven (7) days, the matter shall be escalated to the **404 Community Advisory Board** or designated Lead Mentors for structured mediation.
3. **Amicable Focus:** All dispute resolution must prioritize mutual respect, fairness, team well-being, and project continuity over personal acrimony.

---

### 16. Academic and College Rules

1. **Academic Integrity:** When a project is concurrently submitted for academic evaluation (e.g., college capstone, final-year project, lab course), the team must comply with all applicable institutional guidelines regarding academic honesty and plagiarism.
2. **Clear Boundaries:** Institutional requirements imposed on one student member must not jeopardize the open-source distribution or portfolio rights of other community contributors.

---

### 17. Changes to Agreement

1. **Amendment Process:** This Agreement may only be amended, supplemented, or modified through a written addendum signed by all active contributors and countersigned by a 404 Community Lead.
2. **Version Control:** All revisions must be committed to the project repository with a clear changelog describing the modifications.

---

### 18. No Misrepresentation

1. **Honesty & Transparency:** Contributors must never misrepresent their skills, fake commits, artificially inflate contribution statistics, or misstate their role in the project.
2. **Community Reputation:** Team members agree to represent the project and the 404 Community professionally, refraining from harassment, hate speech, defamation, or malicious behavior both online and during offline community sessions.

---

### 19. Acknowledgement

By signing this document or confirming their agreement electronically, each contributor acknowledges and confirms:
- They have carefully read and understood all nineteen preceding clauses of this **404 Agreement Form**.
- They agree to adhere faithfully to the values, rules, and spirit of **404 Community — From Error to Execution**.
- They understand that this agreement protects their creative rights and the rights of their fellow collaborators.
